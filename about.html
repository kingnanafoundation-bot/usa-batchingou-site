Our story

From the hills of Ndé to fifty American states

Batchingou is a traditional chiefdom in the Ndé department of Cameroon's West Region, one of thirteen Bamiléké chiefdoms in the area. This community exists so that distance from the village never means distance from each other.

Where we come from

The village of Batchingou sits about 17 kilometers from Bangangté, in a landscape of plains, plateaus, and the department's highest peak, Mount Ndoumbock. Its name is often translated as "land of the decision-makers," and its people historically opened their gates to travelers who needed refuge — an identity of protection that the diaspora still carries.

Governed by a chieftaincy tradition that stretches back centuries, the community organizes around three historic sections — Tousseu, Toungwe, and Bangwe — and is known across the region for the Kougang, a traditional dance practiced widely among its people.

Why a USA community

As more sons and daughters of Batchingou settled across the United States for work, study, and family, informal phone trees and group chats grew into something more structured: a registered community able to organize mutual aid, pass on culture to children born abroad, and channel collective support back to the village.

Today the USA Batchingou Community exists to do three things well — take care of members here, stay rooted in identity, and stay useful to home.

Mission

To unite Batchingou natives and friends across the United States in mutual support, cultural preservation, and shared investment in the wellbeing of the home village.

Vision

A diaspora where no member faces hardship alone, where children grow up fluent in where they come from, and where Batchingou's development benefits from its sons and daughters abroad.

Values

Solidarity, transparency, respect for tradition and elders, and open doors to every generation — from founding members to first-generation youth.
